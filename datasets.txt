# Aave Liquidation Prediction Project — Dataset Master List

## 1. MOST IMPORTANT DATASET — Aave V3 Cross-Chain Event Dataset

Name:
Aave V3 Cross-Chain Event-Level Dataset

Source:
Zenodo

URL:
https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.17898640

This is probably the most useful ready-made dataset I found.

It contains more than 50 million structured Aave V3 records across:

* Ethereum
* Arbitrum
* Optimism
* Polygon
* Avalanche
* Base

It covers eight important event types:

* Supply
* Borrow
* Withdraw
* Repay
* LiquidationCall
* FlashLoan
* ReserveDataUpdated
* MintedToTreasury

The data is already decoded and includes blockchain metadata and USD valuations.

The dataset covers each chain from its Aave V3 deployment through October 1, 2025.

Why it is useful:

This single dataset can provide a large part of the core training data for our project.

For our project, the most important files are:

1. Supply
2. Borrow
3. Repay
4. Withdraw
5. LiquidationCall
6. ReserveDataUpdated

The LiquidationCall files are particularly important because they provide the actual liquidation labels/events.

The ReserveDataUpdated files can help reconstruct changes in interest rates and reserve conditions.

The dataset is split into CSV files, generally with a maximum of about 1 million records per file.

Research paper:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.11363

# 37. What Should Become the ML Target?

The most important thing is to construct a label.

For example:

```text
liquidated_within_24h = 1
```

or:

```text
liquidated_within_24h = 0
```

Then our training data becomes:

```text
Position at time T
        +
Market conditions at time T
        +
Aave conditions at time T
        ↓
Will liquidation happen within 24 hours?
        ↓
YES / NO
```

We can later experiment with:

```text
1 hour
6 hours
12 hours
24 hours
48 hours
```

# 41. Very Important Research Rule

Do NOT randomly split individual rows into train/test.

Because blockchain data is time-series data, random splitting can cause data leakage.

A better approach is:

```text
2022 ───────── 2023 ───────── 2024 ───────── 2025
 TRAIN DATA                         TEST DATA
```

For example:

```text
Training:
2022–2024

Validation:
early 2025

Test:
later 2025
```

The exact dates will depend on the dataset.

This is important for making the research academically credible.

# 43. The Most Important Files/Events for Our Project

If we use the large Aave V3 dataset, prioritize:

1. LiquidationCall
2. Borrow
3. Supply
4. Repay
5. Withdraw
6. ReserveDataUpdated

In that order.

LiquidationCall tells us:

"Who actually got liquidated?"

Borrow/Supply/Repay/Withdraw help us reconstruct:

"What was the user's position before liquidation?"

ReserveDataUpdated helps us understand:

"What was happening in the Aave market at that time?"

Market-price data tells us:

"What was happening to the underlying asset?"

Together, these allow us to construct:

```text
USER POSITION
+
MARKET CONDITIONS
+
PROTOCOL CONDITIONS
        ↓
LIQUIDATION RISK
```

# 44. Final Recommendation

The project should NOT be:

"Download one Aave CSV and train an AI."

It should be:

"Construct a historical Aave lending-position dataset by combining Aave V3 event-level data with market-price and protocol-state data, reconstruct borrower positions over time, label positions according to whether liquidation occurs within a future time horizon, and evaluate machine-learning models for early liquidation-risk prediction."

That is the research-grade version of the project.
